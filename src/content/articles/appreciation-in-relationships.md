## Why is expressing appreciation so hard?

You notice things they do. You feel grateful. But saying it out loud feels awkward. Performative. Like you're reading from a greeting card.

So you don't say it. You figure they know. They can tell. Except they can't. Your internal appreciation is invisible to them. All they experience is the silence where gratitude could be.

Over time, unspoken appreciation might as well be no appreciation at all. They can't feel what you don't express.

### Why don't they just know I appreciate them?

Because you've never told them. Or you told them once, five years ago. Or you show it in ways they don't recognize.

People aren't mind readers. And even if they could somehow sense your appreciation, hearing it matters. Being told "I see what you do and I'm grateful" hits different than just assuming it.

> Felt but unexpressed appreciation is like a gift you bought but never gave. It doesn't count.

### What gets in the way of expressing appreciation?

For some people, it was never modeled. Their family didn't say these things. So it feels foreign.

For others, it feels transactional. Like if you say thank you, they'll expect something back. Or like expressing appreciation means admitting you need them.

For many, it's just habit. You got used to not saying it. Now it feels weird to start. The longer you've been silent, the more awkward it feels to break that silence.

### How do I express appreciation without it being weird?

Be specific. "I appreciate you" is generic. "I noticed you got up with the kids even though you were exhausted. Thank you" is specific. Specific feels real. Generic feels scripted.

Don't oversell it. You're not giving a speech. You're noting something you noticed. Keep it short. "Hey, you handled that really well earlier." Done.

Do it in passing. It doesn't need to be a moment. You can say it while doing dishes. While walking by. In a text. The casualness makes it feel natural.

### What if they get weird about it?

They might. If appreciation is new, they might not know how to receive it. That's fine. Say it anyway.

Over time, they'll get used to it. They might even start saying it back. But even if they don't, your expressing it still matters. It builds the fondness system whether they know how to respond or not.

### How often should I express appreciation?

More than you are now. Once a day isn't too much. Once a week is probably the minimum.

The goal isn't to hit a number. The goal is to notice what they do and tell them you noticed. When you catch yourself feeling grateful, say it. That's the whole practice.
