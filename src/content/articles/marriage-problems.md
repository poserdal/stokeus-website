## What's actually wrong with my marriage, and what can I do about it?

Every long relationship hits points where something feels off. You argue more. You feel less close. The person you married feels harder to reach. If you're reading this, you're probably trying to figure out if what you're feeling is normal or if it's a warning sign.

Here's what most marriage problems content won't tell you: almost every marriage problem is fixable if both people want to fix it. The question isn't whether your problems are serious. It's whether you're both willing to do something different.

### Will my marriage problems fix themselves?

Most marriage problems don't get better on their own. The distance doesn't close because you waited it out. The resentment doesn't fade because you stopped bringing it up. What happens instead is the problem becomes the new normal. Six months later you're not arguing about the original thing. You're arguing about the fact that nothing ever changes.

The couples who turn things around stop waiting and start doing specific things to make them better. Not big dramatic gestures. Small repeated actions that show your partner they still matter.

### What does my partner actually need right now?

Most marriage problems aren't actually about the thing you're arguing about. The fight about dishes isn't about dishes. The fight about money isn't really about money. It's about feeling unseen, unheard, or undervalued. Your partner isn't asking you to load the dishwasher. They're asking you to show them they matter.

Ask your partner this question, word for word: "What's one thing I could do this week that would make you feel genuinely cared for?" Not loved in general. Cared for, specifically, this week. Most people have an answer. Most people are never asked.

> The difference between a marriage that works and one that doesn't usually comes down to whether both people are willing to do the thing that feels awkward at first.

### Is keeping score destroying my marriage?

One of the most corrosive patterns in struggling marriages is scorekeeping. You did this so I did that. I always do this and you never do that. Every action becomes a transaction. Every gesture gets weighed against what you didn't get back. At some point the relationship stops feeling like a partnership and starts feeling like a negotiation you're losing.

Scorekeeping guarantees you'll both lose. When you're tracking who's winning, nobody's building anything. **The marriages that last aren't the ones where both people contribute equally every day.** They're the ones where both people give more than they take, most of the time, because they want to.

### What conversation have I been avoiding?

There's probably a conversation you've been putting off. A thing you need to say but haven't said because saying it feels too big or too risky or too likely to start a fight. That conversation is almost certainly the thing standing between where you are now and where you want to be.

You don't need to have it perfectly. You don't need to wait for the right moment. You just need to start it. Say the first sentence. "I've been feeling distant from you and I don't want to feel that way." "I miss us." "I need to talk about something that's been bothering me and I don't want it to turn into a fight." The first sentence is the hardest one. Everything after that gets easier.

### What small moves actually fix marriage problems?

The marriage advice that actually works isn't sexy. It's not a weekend getaway or a big conversation that fixes everything. It's doing small things that matter to your partner, on purpose, repeatedly, even when you're tired and it would be easier not to.

Text them something specific you noticed today. Ask a real question and listen to the whole answer. Touch them when you walk past in the kitchen. Do the one small thing they've mentioned three times. **Good marriages aren't built on never having problems.** They're built on consistently showing up for each other even when it would be easier not to.
