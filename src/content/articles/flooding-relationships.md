## What is flooding in relationships?

Flooding is what happens when your nervous system gets overwhelmed during conflict. Your heart rate goes above 100 bpm. Stress hormones flood your body. Your ability to think clearly, listen, or respond constructively shuts down.

When you're flooded, you can't have a productive conversation. Your brain is in fight-or-flight mode. You're either going to say something you regret or shut down completely. Neither helps.

Gottman found that once you're flooded, nothing useful happens until you calm down. No amount of "just listen to me" or "let's keep talking" will work. The conversation needs to pause.

### How do I know if I'm flooded?

Your body tells you. Racing heart. Shallow breathing. Feeling hot. Tunnel vision. The urge to escape or attack. Thoughts that loop instead of progressing. The sense that you can't take in anything they're saying.

You might also notice you're replaying the last thing they said instead of hearing the current thing. Or that you're formulating your response before they finish talking. Or that you can't remember what was just said.

> Flooding feels like drowning in the conversation. You're in it, but you can't function.

### Why does flooding happen?

Your brain interprets your partner's criticism or contempt as a threat. It triggers the same response as physical danger. Adrenaline. Cortisol. Blood flow away from your brain toward your muscles.

This was useful when threats were predators. It's not useful when the threat is your partner being upset about dishes. But your nervous system doesn't know the difference.

### What do I do when I'm flooded?

Stop the conversation. Not passive aggressively. Actually say: "I'm flooded. I can't hear you right now and I don't want to say something I don't mean. Can we take 20 minutes and come back?"

That's not avoidance. That's self-regulation. You're recognizing that you're physically incapable of having a good conversation in this state.

During the break, don't ruminate. Don't rehearse your argument. Do something that calms your nervous system. Walk. Breathe. Listen to music. The goal is to get your heart rate back down so you can think again.

### What if my partner is the one who's flooded?

Give them space. Pushing harder when someone is flooded doesn't get you heard. It gets you stonewalled or escalated.

Say: "I can see this is a lot. Let's pause and come back when we're both calmer." That's not letting them off the hook. That's acknowledging reality. A flooded brain can't give you what you need.

### Can I prevent flooding before it starts?

Sometimes. Starting conversations softly helps. Avoiding the four horsemen helps. Keeping the overall relationship climate positive helps. If your nervous system feels safe with your partner, it takes more to trigger flooding.

But you can't prevent it entirely. Conflict is activating. The goal isn't to never flood. It's to recognize it faster and recover quicker.
