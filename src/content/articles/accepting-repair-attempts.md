## Why can't I accept my partner's repair attempts?

They try to soften the conflict. You can see them trying. And something in you refuses to let it work. You stay cold. You reject the joke. You pull your hand away.

It's not that you want to keep fighting. It's that accepting the repair feels like letting them off the hook. Like saying what happened was fine. Like giving up your right to be upset.

But that's not what accepting a repair means. It means choosing the relationship over the argument. And that's harder than it sounds when you're still activated.

### What happens when I reject repair attempts?

Your partner learns that trying to de-escalate doesn't work. So they stop trying. Or they try differently, in ways that feel more like pressure than peace. And the fights get longer, meaner, harder to recover from.

Rejecting repair attempts isn't neutral. It teaches your partner that reaching for you is pointless. Eventually, they stop reaching.

> Every rejected repair attempt makes the next one less likely. That's how couples lose the ability to stop their own spirals.

### Why does accepting repair feel like weakness?

Because you're in fight mode. Your nervous system has classified them as a threat. In that state, accepting an olive branch feels like lowering your guard. Your brain screams: don't trust it, they're just trying to end the conversation without addressing the problem.

That instinct made sense on the savannah when threats were physical. It doesn't help in a relationship where the threat is emotional disconnection.

### How do I get better at accepting repair?

Start by noticing when it's happening. Your partner softens their voice. Changes their posture. Makes a bid for connection in the middle of conflict. Catch yourself in the moment of rejection and pause. You don't have to accept it immediately. Just don't actively reject it.

Try this: when they reach for you, don't pull away. When they make a joke, don't glare. When they say "can we start over," don't say "no." Just leave the door open. That's enough for now.

### What if I don't trust their repair attempts?

Maybe you've been here before. They de-escalate, you let it go, and nothing actually changes. The same pattern repeats next week. So now their repair attempts feel like manipulation, a way to end the conversation without fixing anything.

If that's the case, say it. "I want to accept this, but I'm worried we'll just end up here again. Can we actually talk about how to make it different this time?" That's honest. That's not rejecting the repair. That's asking for more.

### Can we talk about repair attempts when we're not fighting?

Yes. And you should. Tell them what actually helps you de-escalate. Maybe it's a specific phrase. Maybe it's space. Maybe it's physical contact. They can't read your mind, especially when both of you are flooded.

Having a shared language for de-escalation makes repair attempts more likely to land. You're building the off-ramp before you need it.
