## What is a bid for connection and why does it matter?

You show your partner a funny video. They glance at it, say nothing, go back to scrolling. You mention something about your day. They nod without looking up. You reach for their hand. They don't reach back.

These moments feel small. They're not.

What just happened is called a bid for connection. It's any attempt to get attention, affection, or acknowledgment from your partner. A question. A comment. A touch. A sigh that's really an invitation to ask what's wrong. Bids happen constantly. Most couples make dozens of them a day without realizing it.

### Why does it matter if my partner misses my bids?

Gottman's research found something brutal. Couples who stayed married turned toward each other's bids 86% of the time. Couples who divorced? 33%. That single behavior predicted relationship survival better than almost anything else they measured.

Turning toward means acknowledging the bid. Looking up. Responding. Engaging, even briefly. Turning away means ignoring it. Not maliciously, usually. Just being too distracted or tired or checked out to notice.

The problem is that turning away doesn't feel like a decision. It feels like nothing. But to your partner, it feels like rejection. Do it enough and they stop reaching.

> You don't drift apart because of one big thing. You drift because of a thousand small moments where nobody reached back.

### What counts as a bid for connection?

Most people think bids are obvious. "Can we talk?" or "I need you." Those are bids. But so is "Look at this." So is "Ugh, traffic was awful." So is walking into the room and sighing.

Bids are often disguised as complaints, observations, or random comments. Your partner says "I'm so tired" and what they're really saying is "Notice me. Ask how I'm doing. Care about my day."

If you're only responding to the explicit requests and ignoring the rest, you're missing most of the bids. And your partner is slowly learning that reaching for you doesn't work.

### How do I turn toward my partner's bids?

You don't have to drop everything. You just have to acknowledge. Look up from your phone. Make eye contact. Say "yeah?" or "that sucks" or "tell me more." Physical touch counts. A hand on their shoulder. Moving closer on the couch.

The bar is not high. You're not solving their problem. You're just showing that you noticed they exist. That's it. That's the whole thing.

### What if I keep missing my partner's bids?

If you realize you've been turning away, say it out loud. "I think I've been checked out lately. I'm going to try to be more present when you talk to me." That one sentence does more than a week of silently trying harder.

And if you're the one whose bids keep getting missed, stop making them subtly and start making them directly. Not passive-aggressively. Just clearly. "Hey, I want your attention for a second." Give your partner a chance to turn toward something they can actually see coming.

Most couples don't fail at connection because they stopped caring. They fail because they stopped noticing.
