## What is a repair attempt?

A repair attempt is anything you do during a conflict to de-escalate it. A joke. A touch. A change of tone. An apology. A silly face. Anything that says: I know this is getting heated and I want to stop before we say things we can't take back.

Gottman found that the success of repair attempts is one of the biggest predictors of whether a relationship will last. Not whether couples fight. Whether they can hit the brakes when a fight starts going sideways.

Happy couples aren't magically more compatible. They're just better at stopping the spiral.

### What does a repair attempt actually look like?

It can be subtle. A softening of your voice. Reaching for their hand. Saying "wait, can we start over?" Or it can be obvious. Making a dumb joke. Using a code word you've agreed on. Saying "I'm sorry, I'm being a jerk right now."

The content doesn't matter as much as the intent. You're signaling: this argument is less important than us. I want to reconnect before we do damage.

> A repair attempt is a bid for connection in the middle of disconnection. It's the hardest time to reach for someone and the most important.

### Why don't my repair attempts work?

Because your partner isn't receiving them. Maybe they're too flooded to notice. Maybe they've learned not to trust your de-escalation because it's felt like manipulation before. Maybe the relationship has enough built-up resentment that any olive branch looks suspicious.

The success of a repair attempt depends on the receiver as much as the sender. If your partner is in fight mode, they might not be able to hear what you're offering.

### How do I make my repair attempts land?

First, pick your moment. If they're at peak intensity, they can't receive anything. Wait for a slight dip. A pause. A breath.

Second, don't make it about being right. "Fine, you win" isn't a repair attempt. It's a surrender that breeds resentment. Try "I don't want to fight about this. Can we figure this out together?" instead.

Third, mean it. If you're offering a repair attempt just to end the conversation so you can go back to being mad, they'll feel that. The attempt has to be genuine.

### What if I'm too angry to make a repair attempt?

Then you're too flooded to be in the conversation at all. Call a timeout. Not an "I'm done talking" that sounds like punishment, but a genuine "I need 20 minutes to calm down and then I want to come back to this."

That's actually a repair attempt in itself. You're saying: I care about this conversation enough to not ruin it by staying in it right now.

### Can we practice repair attempts when we're not fighting?

Yes, and you should. Talk about what works when things are calm. Some couples have a code word that means "this is getting out of hand." Some have a gesture. The point is to establish the pattern before you need it, so it's easier to access when emotions are high.

You're building a shared language for de-escalation. That's not cheating. That's being smart.
