## How is trust actually built in relationships?

Not how you think. Trust isn't built in the big moments. It's built in the small ones. Every time you do what you said you'd do. Every time you're there when they needed you. Every time you choose them over something easier.

Gottman calls these "sliding door moments." Tiny forks where you can either turn toward your partner or turn away. Each one is almost invisible. But they stack up into either a foundation of trust or an absence of it.

The couples who trust each other aren't the ones who've never messed up. They're the ones who've shown up in a thousand small moments.

### What destroys trust?

The obvious things: betrayal, lies, broken promises. But also the small things: not following through, being unreliable, consistently prioritizing other things over the relationship.

Trust erodes slowly. One ignored text. One forgotten promise. One time choosing work over showing up. None of these feel like betrayal. But stacked together, they create a pattern. And your partner starts to learn that they can't count on you.

> Trust is a bank account. You build it in nickels and spend it in dollars. Most people don't notice the balance until it's overdrawn.

### How do I know if my partner trusts me?

Do they come to you with problems? Do they share things they're afraid of? Do they believe you when you say you'll do something?

If they've stopped sharing, stopped relying on you, stopped expecting you to show up, the trust account is low. They've adapted to not counting on you.

### How do I rebuild trust after it's damaged?

Consistently. Not with one big gesture or one apology. With repeated small evidence that you've changed.

This takes time. Maybe a lot of time. They learned not to trust you through repeated experience. They'll learn to trust you again the same way. There are no shortcuts.

The key is to not get defensive when they're skeptical. They're skeptical because you gave them reasons to be. Earn it back one small proof at a time.

### What builds trust faster?

Nothing builds trust faster. That's the hard truth. Trust grows slowly because it should. Your partner is protecting themselves. Trusting too quickly after being hurt would be foolish.

What you can do is be relentlessly consistent. Show up when you say you will. Follow through on small things. Don't overpromise. And when you mess up, own it immediately without defensiveness.

### Why do I struggle to trust my partner?

Maybe they've given you reasons. Maybe they haven't, and you're carrying baggage from somewhere else.

If it's the former, trust needs to be rebuilt through consistent evidence. If it's the latter, that's your work to do. You can't punish this partner for what a previous one did.

Either way, look at the data. Are they actually showing up? Are they actually reliable? Sometimes distrust is justified. Sometimes it's fear masquerading as protection.
