## What does turning away from your partner mean?

Turning away is what happens when your partner makes a bid for connection and you don't respond. Not reject it. Not acknowledge and decline. Just... nothing. You're on your phone. You're distracted. You grunt and keep scrolling.

It doesn't feel like a big deal. That's the problem. One missed bid is fine. But Gottman found that the pattern of turning away is one of the most reliable predictors of relationship failure. Not the fights. The silences.

Because every time you turn away, you're teaching your partner that reaching for you is pointless.

### How is turning away different from turning against?

Turning against is hostile. They show you something and you say "I don't care" or "not now" or roll your eyes. That's rejection. It hurts, but it's obvious.

Turning away is neutral. You just don't respond. You're technically present but functionally absent. It's worse because it's invisible. Your partner can't point to what you did wrong. They just feel the absence.

> Turning away doesn't feel like neglect. It just feels like nothing. That's why couples don't see it until the damage is done.

### Why do I turn away without realizing it?

Because you're tired. Because your phone is interesting. Because they're telling you something you've heard before. Because you don't have the bandwidth and it feels like responding can wait.

None of those feel like neglect. They feel like survival. You're not being a bad partner. You're just being a depleted one. But the effect is the same.

### What happens when turning away becomes a pattern?

Your partner stops reaching. They learn that bids for connection don't work. So they stop making them. The relationship gets quieter. More parallel. Less like a partnership and more like a living arrangement.

By the time you notice, the distance feels normal. You've both adapted to the lack of connection. Reversing it feels impossible because neither of you remembers how to reach anymore.

### How do I stop turning away?

Start by noticing. When they say something, check: am I actually responding? Or am I half-present?

You don't have to drop everything for every comment. But you do have to acknowledge. Make eye contact. Say something. Signal that you heard them.

If you can't engage right now, say that. "I want to hear this but I'm in the middle of something. Give me two minutes?" That's turning toward. That's respecting the bid while being honest about your capacity.

### What if I've been turning away for years?

Then start turning toward now. You can't undo the past but you can change the pattern. One acknowledged bid at a time.

It'll feel weird at first. They might be suspicious. But if you keep showing up, the pattern will shift. Slowly. The way it got bad.
