## What makes a problem solvable?

A solvable problem is situational, not personality-based. It's about a specific issue that can be addressed with a decision, a system, or a change in behavior. Once you handle it, it stays handled.

"We need to figure out how to split chores fairly" is solvable. "You're a lazy person" is not. "We disagree about what time to leave for things" is solvable. "You're always inconsiderate" is not.

The key difference: solvable problems don't have a dream underneath. They're just logistics that need working out.

### Why do I treat solvable problems like personality conflicts?

Because by the time you bring it up, you're frustrated. And frustrated brains don't distinguish between "this thing is annoying" and "you are annoying." The problem gets wrapped in emotion and suddenly you're not solving anything. You're just venting.

The shift from solvable to unsolvable happens in your language. When you say "you never" or "you always," you've made it about their character. When you say "this specific thing happened and here's what I need," you've kept it fixable.

> Most solvable problems become unsolvable because we wait too long to address them. By then, the resentment has contaminated everything.

### How do I solve a solvable problem?

Start soft. Don't lead with accusation. "I've noticed we keep bumping into this issue and I want to figure out a system that works for both of us."

Stay specific. Name the actual problem, not the pattern you've invented around it. "I feel stressed when I don't know what's for dinner until 7pm" is specific. "You never plan anything" is not.

Propose something. Don't just complain. Bring a solution to discuss. "What if we planned meals on Sunday?" gives your partner something to respond to.

Be willing to adjust. Your first solution might not work. That's fine. The goal is to keep iterating until you find something sustainable.

### What if my partner gets defensive when I bring up a problem?

Check your approach. Are you actually presenting it as a solvable problem, or are you sliding into criticism? "I need help with this" lands differently than "You never help."

If you've approached it gently and they're still defensive, name that. "I'm trying to solve this together, not blame you. Can we try again?"

### What if we've tried to solve it before and it didn't work?

Then the solution didn't fit. Not every problem is solved on the first try. Go back and figure out why it failed. Was the system too complicated? Did someone forget? Was there a hidden reason it didn't work?

Treat failures as data. What would make this easier? What would make it stick? Keep adjusting until you find what works.

### When should I stop trying to solve something?

When you realize it's not actually solvable. If the same problem keeps coming back despite multiple genuine attempts, it might be perpetual. The underlying issue might be a personality difference, not a logistics issue.

That doesn't mean you give up. It means you shift from solving to managing. Different skill, different approach.
