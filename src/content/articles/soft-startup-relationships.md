## What is a soft startup?

A soft startup is how you begin a difficult conversation. Gottman found that 96% of the time, conversations end the way they begin. If you start harsh, you'll end harsh. If you start soft, you have a chance of actually getting somewhere.

A harsh startup sounds like accusation. "You never listen to me." "Why do you always do this?" "I can't believe you forgot again." Your partner hears attack. They get defensive. Now you're fighting about the fight instead of the issue.

A soft startup sounds like a request. "I need to talk about something that's been bothering me. Can we find a time?" "I felt hurt when this happened. Can I tell you about it?" Your partner hears an invitation. The door stays open.

### Why is starting soft so hard?

Because by the time you bring something up, you're already frustrated. You've been sitting on it for hours or days. The emotion has built up. And when you finally say something, all that frustration comes out in the first sentence.

You're not trying to attack them. You're just full. And full spills over.

> The moment you most want to start harsh is the moment it matters most to start soft.

### What does a soft startup actually sound like?

It has four parts. Start with "I" instead of "you." Describe the situation without blame. Say how you felt. Make a specific request.

Instead of: "You never help with bedtime and I'm exhausted."

Try: "I've been handling bedtime alone for a while. I'm really tired. Can we figure out a way to share it?"

Same problem. Different approach. The second one gives your partner something to respond to that isn't their own defense.

### What if I'm too angry to start soft?

Then don't start yet. A conversation started in anger will end in damage. Wait until you've calmed down enough to approach it without leading with attack.

That doesn't mean stuffing it down. It means choosing when to engage strategically. "I need to talk about something but I'm too heated right now. Can we talk in an hour?" That's not avoidance. That's setup.

### What if they start harsh with me?

You have two choices. Match their energy and guarantee a fight. Or try to redirect.

"I can tell you're really upset about this. Can we start over so I can actually hear what you need?" That's not being a pushover. That's refusing to let their harsh startup dictate the conversation.

Sometimes it works. Sometimes they're too flooded. But at least you didn't add fuel.

### How do I get better at soft startups?

Practice before you need it. When you're not in conflict, think about how you'd bring up something difficult. Script the first sentence. "I want to talk about something that's been on my mind. It's not an attack. I just need to feel heard."

The more you rehearse soft starts when calm, the more accessible they'll be when you're activated. You're building a new default.
