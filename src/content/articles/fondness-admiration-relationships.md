## What is the fondness and admiration system?

Gottman calls it the "fondness and admiration system" but it's simpler than that. It's whether you still like your partner. Not love. Like. Do you still enjoy them? Respect them? Find them interesting? Think they're a good person?

Fondness is the feeling that your partner is someone you're glad to be with. Admiration is the belief that they have qualities worth respecting. Together, they're the antidote to contempt.

If you've lost fondness and admiration, you're vulnerable. Not because you're doomed, but because without that foundation, small annoyances become evidence. You stop giving them the benefit of the doubt.

### How do I know if I've lost fondness?

Notice how you think about them. When they tell a story at a party, do you think "this is boring" or "that's charming"? When they make a mistake, do you think "typical" or "happens to everyone"? When something good happens to them, are you genuinely happy or secretly resentful?

The answers reveal whether you still have positive sentiment override. That's the state where your overall feeling about them is positive enough that small negatives don't register as threats.

If you've flipped to negative sentiment override, everything they do gets filtered through annoyance. Even neutral things become evidence against them.

> Contempt doesn't start with the eye roll. It starts with quietly building a case against them in your head.

### What happens when fondness dies?

Criticism escalates to contempt. Small conflicts become referendums on their character. You start telling yourself a story where they're the problem. "They've always been like this." "They'll never change."

Once that narrative takes hold, it's hard to see counter-evidence. Your brain confirms what it already believes.

### Can fondness come back once it's gone?

Yes. But not passively. Not by waiting for them to become likable again.

You rebuild fondness by deliberately noticing what's good. What do you appreciate about them? What did they do well today? What's something they're good at that you've taken for granted? What would you miss if they were gone?

These questions feel forced at first. That's the point. You're counteracting a negativity bias that's been building.

### How do I show fondness and admiration?

Tell them. Not in grand declarations. In small, specific moments. "I noticed you handled that really well." "I like how you are with your friends." "You made me laugh today."

These aren't compliments. They're deposits into the fondness system. Each one reminds your brain that you're with someone you actually like.

### What if I try to find things to admire and can't?

Then the relationship is in trouble. Not unsalvageable trouble. But trouble.

If you genuinely can't think of anything you respect about your partner, you need to ask yourself hard questions. Has contempt taken over? Are you seeing them clearly or through resentment? Is there anything left to work with?

Sometimes the answer is that the fondness died for real reasons. Sometimes it's that you've lost sight of it under accumulated frustration. The first step is being honest about which one it is.
