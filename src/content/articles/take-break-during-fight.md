## Why is taking a break so hard to do right?

Because most breaks don't feel like pauses. They feel like punishment. When someone walks away from an argument, the other person usually doesn't think "they're regulating their nervous system." They think "they're abandoning me in the middle of something important."

The difference between a helpful break and a harmful one isn't whether you take it. It's how you frame it, what you do during it, and whether you actually come back.

### What's the wrong way to take a break?

Storming off. Slamming doors. "Whatever, I'm done." Leaving without saying when you'll return. Going silent and staying silent. Using the break to punish them by withholding connection.

These aren't breaks. They're stonewalling dressed up as self-care. And they make things worse because now there are two problems: the original issue and the abandonment your partner just experienced.

### What's the right way to take a break?

Name what's happening. "I'm getting flooded and I can't hear you right now. I need 20 minutes to calm down and then I want to come back to this."

Set a specific time. Not "later." Not "when I'm ready." Give them something concrete. "Can we pick this up in half an hour?" That tells them you're not escaping. You're pausing.

Mean it. Actually come back. A break that doesn't have a return isn't a break. It's avoidance.

> A break is a gift to the conversation. But only if you return with more capacity than you left with.

### What do I do during the break?

Not what you think. Don't use the time to build your case. Don't rehearse everything you're going to say when you return. Don't scroll your phone while stewing.

The break is for physiological recovery. Walk. Breathe deeply. Do something that shifts your state. The goal is to get your heart rate under 100 bpm so you can actually think and listen again.

Gottman says it takes at least 20 minutes for stress hormones to clear. If you come back sooner, you're probably still flooded. Give yourself the full time.

### What if my partner won't let me take a break?

Some people pursue when their partner withdraws. If that's your dynamic, you need to talk about it outside of conflict. Explain that you're not abandoning them. You're protecting the conversation.

You might need to agree on a protocol in advance. "When I say 'I need a break,' I'm not shutting you out. I'm making sure I can show up better when we return." Having that shared understanding makes breaks feel collaborative instead of combative.

### What do I say when I come back?

Start soft. "Okay, I'm calmer now. Thanks for giving me that space. Can we try again?" That acknowledges them, shows you're ready, and sets a different tone.

Don't just restart where you left off. The energy is different now. Use that. Maybe you can see something you couldn't see when you were flooded.
