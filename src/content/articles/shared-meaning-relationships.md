## What is shared meaning in a relationship?

Shared meaning is the sense that you and your partner are building something together. It's the story you tell about who you are as a couple. The values you share. The rituals you've created. The vision of your future that you're working toward together.

Gottman puts shared meaning at the top of his Sound Relationship House. It's not the foundation, but it's the reason the house exists. Without it, you're just cohabitating.

Couples with shared meaning feel like partners. Couples without it feel like roommates managing a joint operation.

### Why does shared meaning matter?

Because it gives context to the hard stuff. When you know why you're together and what you're building, the daily friction matters less. You can tolerate more because you see it in service of something larger.

Without shared meaning, every conflict is existential. "Why am I even doing this?" becomes a question instead of a rhetorical complaint.

> Shared meaning is the story you tell yourself about why this relationship matters. Without it, you're just getting through days together.

### How do I know if we have shared meaning?

Ask yourself: do you know what you're building together? Can you articulate your shared values? Do you have rituals that are yours? Do you have a sense of where you're going as a couple?

If the answer is no, that's not a death sentence. It's just a gap. Most couples never explicitly create shared meaning. They assume it happens automatically. It doesn't.

### How do we create shared meaning?

Start with rituals. What do you do together that's just yours? Morning coffee. Sunday breakfast. The way you say goodbye. If you don't have rituals, create them. They're the small structures that signal "we're a we."

Then talk about values. What matters to you both? What kind of home do you want to have? What role does family play? Work? Adventure? These conversations feel abstract but they reveal whether you're pointed in the same direction.

Finally, talk about the future. What are you building toward? What do you want your life to look like in five years? Ten? Do your visions overlap?

### What if we don't agree on shared meaning?

That's where negotiation comes in. You don't have to want exactly the same things. You have to want compatible things. And you have to care about their vision enough to make room for it.

If you want to stay in the city and they want to move to the country, that's not necessarily fatal. But you have to actually talk about it. Find out what's underneath each preference. Look for the overlap.

### Can shared meaning change over time?

Yes. And it should. The story you told about your relationship in your twenties won't fit in your forties. Kids change it. Career changes change it. You change.

The couples who last aren't the ones who lock in a vision and never revisit it. They're the ones who keep checking in. Keep asking: who are we now? What are we building next?
