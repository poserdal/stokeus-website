## What is a harsh startup?

A harsh startup is how most frustrated people begin difficult conversations. It's leading with criticism, contempt, or accusation. "You always..." "You never..." "Why can't you just..." "I can't believe you..."

The moment your partner hears those words, their nervous system classifies you as a threat. Walls go up. Listening stops. They're not hearing your point anymore. They're building their defense.

Gottman found that conversations that begin with harsh startups almost never end productively. You've lost before you started.

### Why do I keep starting harsh when I don't mean to?

Because you've been sitting on the problem. By the time you say something, the frustration has fermented into resentment. The first words that come out are the overflow.

You don't intend to attack. You intend to finally be heard. But the urgency of being heard makes you loud. And loud sounds like attack.

> A harsh startup is what happens when you wait too long to say something. The pressure builds until it explodes.

### What makes a startup harsh?

Starting with "you" instead of "I." Using absolutes like "always" and "never." Leading with the accusation instead of the feeling. Making it about their character instead of the specific thing that happened.

"You never think about how this affects me" is harsh. "I felt overlooked when this happened" is soft. Same underlying issue. Completely different reception.

### What happens after a harsh startup?

One of two things. They get defensive and now you're fighting about the fight. Or they shut down and you get nothing at all.

Either way, you don't get heard. Which is ironic, because getting heard was the whole point.

The frustrating part is that you might be completely right about the issue. But being right doesn't matter if your approach makes them incapable of hearing it.

### How do I recover if I already started harsh?

Stop. Acknowledge it. "I'm sorry, that came out wrong. Can I start over?"

That's a repair attempt. It shows you noticed what happened and you care about doing it differently. Sometimes they'll give you another chance. Sometimes they're too activated and you need to take a break.

Either way, you've interrupted the spiral. That's worth something.

### How do I break the harsh startup habit?

Catch yourself before you speak. When you feel the urge to say something, pause. Take a breath. Ask yourself: am I about to start with "you"? Am I about to use "always" or "never"?

Then reframe. What am I actually feeling? What specifically happened? What do I need?

It feels slow at first. That's fine. You're rewiring a reflex. The more you practice catching it, the more automatic the softer approach becomes.
