## Why don't grand gestures fix my relationship?

You planned the vacation. You bought the flowers. You wrote the card. You did the thing you were supposed to do.

And it worked. For a day or two. Then you went back to normal. The distance crept back in. The disconnection returned. And now you're wondering what else you're supposed to do.

Here's what nobody tells you: grand gestures can't compensate for what happens the other 364 days. If your daily interactions are neutral or negative, one big positive moment won't shift the average enough to matter.

Relationships aren't saved by special occasions. They're built in the boring moments.

### What makes small things more powerful than big ones?

A weekend getaway is one data point. A daily "thinking of you" text is 365 data points. Your brain keeps a running average of how the relationship feels. One amazing moment gets averaged in, but so do the three hundred mediocre ones.

Gottman calls this "small things often." It's the idea that consistent small positives do more for a relationship than occasional big ones. A hand on their back when you walk past. Asking about their day and actually listening. Noticing when they seem off and mentioning it.

None of these feel romantic. That's not the point. They feel present. And presence, stacked up over time, is what makes someone feel loved.

> The couples who stay close aren't doing more. They're doing small things more consistently.

### What counts as a small positive?

It's simpler than you think. Eye contact when they're talking. A real compliment about something specific. Sitting close to them on the couch. Saying "thank you" for something you usually take for granted. Laughing at something they said. Texting them something that has nothing to do with logistics.

You don't have to come up with new material every day. You just have to notice them. Pay attention. Acknowledge that they exist and that you're glad about it.

### Why do I default to grand gestures instead?

Because grand gestures feel more like effort. They're visible. Countable. You can point to the reservation you made and feel like you did something.

Small gestures don't feel like work. Which is why it's easy to skip them. You think: I didn't do anything today, but we're going to that concert next month, so it's fine. Except the concert doesn't offset the three weeks of coasting that came before it.

### How do I build the habit of small things?

Pick one moment in your day where you'll add a deliberate positive. Maybe it's when you wake up. Maybe it's when you get home from work. Maybe it's right before you go to sleep.

Make it specific and repeatable. "Every morning, I'll say one thing I appreciate about them." Not because you feel overwhelming gratitude. Because you're training yourself to notice the good.

It'll feel forced at first. That's normal. The point isn't to feel romantic. It's to build a pattern that compounds over time.

### What if my partner doesn't notice the small things?

They might not notice consciously. But their nervous system does. Over time, the feeling of being in the relationship will shift. They'll feel more connected without knowing why. The temperature will rise.

And if you're keeping track hoping they'll reciprocate, stop. This isn't a transaction. It's climate control. You do the small things because they make the relationship better, not because you're waiting for credit.

If you need to talk about reciprocity, talk about it. But separately. Don't let the small positives become a scorecard.
