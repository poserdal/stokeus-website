## Why is contempt so dangerous in relationships?

You can yell at your partner and still respect them. You can fight hard and make up. You can say things you regret and repair the damage.

But contempt is different.

Contempt communicates that your partner is beneath you. That you don't just disagree with them. You look down on them. The eye-roll. The dismissive snort. The mocking tone when you repeat what they said. The sarcastic "yeah, okay" that makes it clear you think they're an idiot.

Gottman found that contempt is the single strongest predictor of divorce. Stronger than any other conflict pattern. Stronger than how often you fight or what you fight about.

### What makes contempt different from anger?

Anger says "I'm upset about something you did." Contempt says "I think less of you as a person."

When you're angry, you're still treating your partner as an equal you're fighting with. When you're contemptuous, you've decided you're above them. You're not engaging with their point. You're dismissing their right to have a point at all.

That's why it's so corrosive. You can resolve a disagreement. You can apologize for harsh words. But you can't easily un-communicate "I think you're worthless."

> Contempt isn't how you fight. It's how you've started to see them. That shift is what destroys relationships.

### Why do I feel contemptuous toward my partner?

Contempt doesn't come from nowhere. It usually builds from accumulated resentment. The things that bother you that never got addressed. The disappointments that stacked up. The moments where you felt unseen or unappreciated and swallowed it instead of saying it.

Over time, those unspoken frustrations curdle into contempt. You're not just mad about this one thing. You've started keeping a mental list of everything wrong with them. And once you have that list, every new frustration gets added to the pile instead of dealt with on its own.

### How do I stop being contemptuous?

You can't white-knuckle your way out of contempt. Telling yourself to stop rolling your eyes doesn't address why you want to roll them.

Start by dealing with the resentment. What are you actually mad about? Not the small thing that triggered the eye-roll. The bigger thing underneath it. Say that out loud. Have the uncomfortable conversation you've been avoiding.

Then deliberately rebuild respect. Make a list of things you genuinely admire about your partner. Not things you're supposed to admire. Things you actually do. When you catch yourself thinking something contemptuous, counter it with something from that list.

### What if my partner is contemptuous toward me?

If you're on the receiving end of contempt, it hurts like hell. Being looked down on by the person who's supposed to love you most is devastating.

You can't control whether they respect you. But you can tell them what you're experiencing. "When you roll your eyes at me, it makes me feel like you don't respect me at all." Not in the middle of a fight. Later, when things are calm.

If they dismiss that too, you have important information about where this relationship stands. Contempt that one person won't acknowledge or address doesn't go away. It gets worse.

You deserve to be with someone who sees you as an equal. Even on the hard days.
