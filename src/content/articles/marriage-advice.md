## Why doesn't most marriage advice work?

Most marriage advice is designed to sound profound, not to change behavior. "Communicate more." "Prioritize quality time." All of it technically true. None of it specific enough to do tonight. You read it, nod along, and then nothing changes because knowing something and doing something are completely different problems.

Here's what actually works.

### Does my marriage need different things now than before?

People change. Stress changes people. Kids change people. What worked in year two might not land in year seven. Ask your partner what they need right now. Not last year. Right now. Most couples never ask directly. They assume they know. They're usually wrong.

### What's the most important thing I can do for my marriage?

Grand gestures feel good. They're also one-offs. The couples who stay deeply connected kept showing up in small, specific ways, every week. **Consistency beats intensity.** A text that shows you're thinking of them. A question that proves you were listening. A moment where you choose them over your phone.

One small thing, done consistently, rewires a relationship faster than a dozen big moments.

> The difference between a good marriage and a great one isn't passion. It's patterns.

### How do I learn what makes my partner feel loved?

Love languages are real, but most people get them wrong. It's not just about knowing your partner prefers acts of service. It's about knowing which specific acts actually land. Doing the dishes might mean nothing. Handling the thing they've been dreading for two weeks might mean everything.

Ask what makes them feel genuinely cared for. Then do that thing. Not once. Repeatedly.

### Should I keep track of who does more in my marriage?

Relationships aren't fifty-fifty. Some weeks you're carrying ninety percent. Some weeks they are. **The moment you start tracking contributions is the moment you stop being a team.**

If you feel like you're doing more than your share, that's real. But the solution isn't scorekeeping. It's a conversation about what's not working.

### How do I fight better in my marriage?

Conflict doesn't kill marriages. Bad conflict does. The couples who last fight in a way that resolves something instead of just venting frustration. Say what you actually need instead of hoping they'll guess. Fight about the real issue, not the surface one.

Most fights aren't about what they're about. The fight about dishes is usually about feeling unseen. The fight about money is usually about control or fear. Figure out what you're actually fighting about.

### Why do marriages drift apart?

The most common pattern in struggling marriages isn't drama. It's drift. You stop putting in effort because you assume the relationship will be fine. You prioritize work, kids, friends, everything else. **A marriage that doesn't get attention doesn't stay strong. It just gets quieter until you're strangers living in the same house.**

Marriages don't fail because people stop loving each other. They fail because people stop acting like it.
